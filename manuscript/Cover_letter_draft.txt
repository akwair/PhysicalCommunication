Dear Editors of Physical Communication,

Please consider our manuscript, "Residual-noise calibration in off-grid OFDM:
Leakage bias, temporal correlation, and cyclic-prefix controls", for publication
as a research article.

The paper quantifies when averaging a pilot residual becomes dominated by
channel leakage rather than sampling variance. A covariance-based crossover
connects fractional-delay mismatch, subspace width, and the number of
independent calibration blocks. A sensitivity study with two pilot budgets
shows why reducing leakage can still increase calibration error when too few
residual dimensions remain. Correlated-gain analysis identifies the separate
effect of temporal dependence on the averaging window.

A paired experiment connects the calibration diagnosis to reconstruction:
replacing the residual statistic with the established CP direct-difference
statistic improves the same continuous-delay receiver by 2.98 and 4.25 dB
in NMSE at 20 dB SNR for two fractional-delay profiles. The experiment retains
the physical dependence between CP and FFT noise. Continuous-delay inference
and the CP statistic are attributed to their original sources; their use as
controlled components isolates the effect of calibration. Dense-channel
controls and a pilot-domain VALSE check further delimit the interpretation.
The manuscript addresses physical-layer OFDM channel estimation and receiver
analysis through a quantitative diagnostic and reproducible simulation study.

The study uses simulated observations. Code, seeds, per-group metric outputs,
and figure sources are prepared in a reproducibility package. OpenAI Codex was
used for debugging assistance, as stated in the declaration.

research grant.

The authors confirm that this work is original, has not been published
previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere.
