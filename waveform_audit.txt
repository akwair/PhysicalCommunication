Finite-CP fractional-delay audit (2026-11-13)
Scope: independent scientific model check, not a paper or novelty claim.
Files: waveform_model.py and test_waveform_model.py.

1. What is physically specified
The continuous complex-baseband OFDM symbol is a finite sum of tones:
 s_b(t) = N^(-1/2) sum_k X_b[k] exp(j 2 pi q_k t/N), -C <= t < N,
where q_k=N*fftfreq(N), and consecutive symbols occupy disjoint windows of
length N+C. Time and delays are measured in base-rate sample periods. Thus
for N=64, q=[0,...,31,-32,...,-1]. The -32 Nyquist tone is explicitly chosen;
choosing +32 describes a different continuous waveform between sample times.
Each symbol includes its own finite cyclic prefix. The burst is zero outside
its finite duration; the simulator never wraps the full burst around itself.

The ideal channel is r(t)=sum_p g_p s(t-tau_p), tau_p>=0. Each batch entry has
one channel fixed over its burst, a valid special case of block-static fading.
Sampling is exactly synchronized. There is no intra-symbol time variation,
CFO, phase noise, TX/RX filter, timing error, RF impairment, or channel coding.
Rectangular switching has spectral sidelobes: the overall transmitted burst
is not globally bandlimited. This check does NOT establish a filtered analog
TX/RX chain, a hardware model, or 3GPP compliance. Adding pulse shaping or
analog filtering requires treating its effective memory and receive timing.

2. Independent time-domain construction and scaling
Signed frequency bins are embedded in a length N*S spectrum, with all other
bins zero. A unitary IFFT times sqrt(S) produces samples of the tone sum at
t=m/S. Every Sth useful sample is exactly ifft(X,norm='ortho'). The useful
symbol's mean sample power is mean(abs(X)^2); its oversampled energy divided
by S is sum(abs(X)^2). The finite CP adds duration and energy. No CP overhead
is hidden in an Eb/N0 claim; the research v parameter is a per-bin noise
variance with nominal E|H[k]|^2=1 and unit-energy symbols.

Every tau must satisfy S*tau integer, within roundoff. Unsupported delays
raise an error rather than being rounded. With S=20, delays [0,7.35,11.2]
are represented exactly as [0,147,224] high-rate samples. The concatenated
burst is delayed by literal linear array shifts, summed across paths,
sampled every S positions, stripped of each CP, then transformed by a
unitary FFT. The transmit/channel path never calls channel_response or
multiplies X by a per-subcarrier response.

For the ideal delay-only channel, C>=max(tau) ensures all delayed FFT input
samples remain inside the current symbol's CP extension. Therefore
 H[k]=sum_p g_p exp(-j 2 pi fftfreq(N)[k] tau_p), Y[k]=X[k]H[k].
This equality follows for any real delay under the specified continuous
waveform. The numerical construction checks representable delays on a finite
oversampling grid. CP=16 is sufficient for max(tau)=11.2; CP=8 is insufficient.
With a changed previous OFDM symbol, the current output changes only in the
insufficient-CP test, directly demonstrating inter-symbol interference.

Noise is omitted from the waveform check to isolate this equivalence.
Independent CN(0,v) noise at the base-rate receiver samples remains CN(0,v I)
after the unitary FFT. The simulator does not assign a physical analog PSD to
independent oversampled noise or model receive filtering of that noise.

3. Unsigned versus signed frequencies
Original noise_study.fourier uses k/N for k=0,...,N-1. Centered baseband uses
f_k=k/N for k<N/2 and f_k=k/N-1 for k>=N/2 (even N). On the negative half,
 exp(-j 2 pi f_k tau) = exp(-j 2 pi k tau/N) exp(+j 2 pi tau).
The two agree for integer tau, but differ for fractional tau. Integer-only
tests cannot select the physical continuous-time convention. For k=48,
N=64, tau=0.5, a physical -1/4 tone produces exp(+j pi/4); the unsigned
formula produces its negative. A single-tone waveform test detects this.

The unsigned response is not intrinsically impossible: it describes an
alternate uncentered [0,Fs) complex-tone synthesis. It must not be combined
with centered baseband interpolation without changing the interpretation.
The new function delay_atoms uses the centered convention explicitly.

4. Assessment of the existing leakage-bias claim
The projection identity is valid in either convention:
 E[vhat]=v+tr(Q B P B^H)/(M-L), Q=I-A(A^H A)^(-1)A^H.
Fractional physical delays need not lie in the span of L integer-delay atoms,
even when the physical maximum delay lies below the CP. Thus sufficient CP
does not remove integer-subspace model leakage. The claim is a standard
projection identity and carries no novelty implication.

For the CURRENT configuration N=64, pilots=0,4,...,60, independent zero-mean
path gains with diagonal P, this additive bias is exactly the same under
the two frequency conventions. Let R shift the M=16 pilot rows by 8 places.
Then B_signed=R B_unsigned diag(exp(j pi tau_p)), while R A=A D with
D_ll=(-1)^l. Therefore Q commutes with R, and diagonal P cancels the per-path
unit phases in the trace. This proves equality, which is also checked by
independent numerical projections. Do not generalize it to arbitrary pilot
masks, missing subcarriers, or correlated path coefficients.

For delays [0,7.35,11.2], powers [0.5,0.3,0.2]:
 L=12: additive E[vhat]-v = 0.07774734946760602.
 L=15: additive E[vhat]-v = 0.024328667084120167.
At v=0.01 the expected vhat/v is about 8.774735 and 3.432867, respectively.
Consequently the original high-SNR residual bias is NOT an artifact that
disappears when the frequency indexing is corrected. Existing numeric
rankings are still model-specific; this audit does not rerun or certify them.
